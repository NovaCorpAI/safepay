How the system protects itself and your data

Evidence nobody can rewrite

Every decision is a link in a hash chain, HMAC-signed; editing or dropping one breaks every hash after it, and the ledger re-verifies the whole chain on demand. Seals and prechecks carry Ed25519 receipts verifiable offline against our published key.

Rules signed by the operator

The policy is signed in the operator's browser with a non-extractable P-256 key and re-verified on every payment; an edited policy blocks everything until it is signed again.

Isolation and keys

Each agent key names one account; a caller sees only its own policy and checks. Keys are shown once and stored only as a hash. All traffic is HTTPS.

Least data

The connector moves no money and never sees card or bank credentials. Purchase checks for Impulsa keep only a hash of the business in the public chain, no names, document numbers or amounts.

One intent, one charge

PayPal de-duplicates a request that carries a PayPal-Request-Id. The PayPal Agent Toolkit's Python client (1.1.0 and 1.11.0) sends none, so an agent retrying after a timeout creates a second order or charge. SafePay's gate adds a request id derived from the intent to every money-moving toolkit call, writes it into the chain link, and its own PayPal client has always sent one. We sent the fix upstream too: paypal/agent-toolkit#124, open.

Incidents

A security incident involving user data is reported to affected users and platform partners within 48 hours of discovery. Report a vulnerability to security@novacorplabs.com or through GitHub's private reporting.

Every attacker we design for, what stops them and what does not yet, is in SECURITY.md.
